Dear Commissioner Bratti:

I am writing to apply for the Assistant Commissioner of Health and Human Services role supporting your oversight across Public Health, Veterans' Services, Elder Affairs, the Disabilities Commission, the Public Library, the Youth Commission, civic inclusion work, and Recreation. I have lived in Plymouth nearly my entire life, apart from education and military training, and I am deeply invested in the health, dignity, safety, and long-term resilience of this town. As a Plymouth native, environmentalist, caretaker, veteran of service at Joint Base Cape Cod, and public servant with direct experience in community crisis conditions, I believe I am strongly suited to help serve the Department and the residents who depend on it.

My strongest fit is the combination of local commitment, public health training, operational accountability, and evidence-based decision support. I am completing my Master of Public Health practicum and have been named an incoming Dartmouth Scholar in recognition of my commitment to public health. My current work focuses on turning data, artificial intelligence, machine learning, and geospatial tools into practical solutions for rural and global communities. That work is already moving from research into implementation: an ArcGIS-based public health delivery product I helped develop is being positioned for use in McDowell County, West Virginia, to support WASH planning and community health decision-making.

I also bring practical experience in environments where policy, documentation, judgment, and cross-functional coordination matter. As a Regional Disaster Dispatcher with the American Red Cross, I supported incident triage, team activation, escalation workflows, and communications needed to align resources with urgent community needs. As a Correction Officer with the Massachusetts Department of Correction, I gained direct exposure to the human realities of the Massachusetts opioid addiction crisis, including its effects on families, public safety, health systems, behavioral health needs, and local communities. My Air Force service further strengthened disciplined information handling, mission support, and work under clearly defined legal and policy constraints.

The Plymouth role is especially compelling because it sits at the intersection of public health, aging, disability access, veterans' services, youth development, recreation, civic inclusion, and municipal operations. I would be prepared to support your leadership by strengthening service planning, improving data tracking, organizing grant and compliance workflows, monitoring state and federal policy developments, and helping translate evidence into practical programs. I would also approach the ADA Coordinator function with the seriousness it deserves: as a core municipal access, equity, communication, and implementation responsibility that should be integrated across departments rather than treated as a narrow compliance task.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my roots in Plymouth, public health systems training, AI and machine-learning expertise, emergency-response experience, and accountable public-sector service can support the Town of Plymouth's Human Services Department. Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

Paul A. Skeffington
